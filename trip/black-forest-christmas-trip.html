Why this route

Why it's built this way
  1. A Christmas market every evening, and you sleep close to it. Stuttgart and Colmar are walk-to-the-hotel evenings, and Ravenna is a 25-minute drive back to Freiburg, so the driver keeps the mulled wine light that night. The only drive after dark is Saturday's trip to the airport.
  2. Stuttgart on its opening night. One of Germany's biggest and oldest markets opens on Wednesday 25 at 18:00, the evening you land. A nap in the afternoon covers the overnight flight.
  3. The spa on Thursday, when you're rested. Baden-Baden sits on the way from Stuttgart to Colmar. Its market only opens on the 26th and is the least special of your markets, so skipping it costs little.
  4. Colmar twice, with no drive home. Thursday night with the lights, Friday morning with Petite Venise almost empty. A weekday also means easier crowds and parking.
  5. Freiburg is the hub for Friday. It's under an hour from Colmar via Breisach, 25 minutes from the Ravenna shuttle, and 40 minutes from Europa-Park.
  6. Sleeping in Freiburg on Friday gives you a real city evening and more hotel choice than the park hotels. Leave around 10:15 on Saturday to be at the gates for the 11:00 opening. The 28th is the first day of the winter season and a Saturday, so it will be busy.
The Saturday question

Getting back to Frankfurt
Recommended

Drive to an airport hotel Saturday evening

About 2 hours, leaving around 18:00, which is early evening. Switch drivers halfway. You sleep a full night and wake up at the terminal.

Sleep near the park, drive Sunday morning

Avoids the Saturday evening drive, but with an 11:00 flight and EL AL's 3-hour rule you'd leave Rust at about 05:45. The roads are empty (no trucks on German Sundays), but you lose the sleep.

Stop halfway (Baden-Baden or Karlsruhe)

The worst of both: you still drive on Saturday night, add a hotel change, and leave at about 06:15 on Sunday.

Good to know

Practical notes and small extras

Borders

France and Germany are both in Schengen, so there's no routine passport control. Germany does spot checks, so keep passports in the car.

Parking in Colmar

On a Thursday the central garages (Lacarre, Rapp, Mairie) should be fine. Check whether your hotel has its own parking first.

Skip Strasbourg

It needs a French Crit'Air sticker and has the region's heaviest traffic and crowds. Colmar gives more charm for less effort.

Titisee instead of Freiburg

If you'd rather see a lake than a city on Friday, walk by Lake Titisee and take the Ravenna shuttle from the Titisee Park & Ride.

Friedrichsbad over Caracalla

Caracalla is a very good modern spa. Friedrichsbad's 17-station Roman-Irish ritual under a 19th-century dome exists only here.

Eguisheim

A village built in rings around a castle, 10 minutes from Colmar, with its own small market. Add an hour on the way in if you have energy.

What to eat and drink

In Alsace: white mulled wine (vin chaud blanc), flammekueche and bredele biscuits. At the German markets: Feuerzangenbowle.

Opening nights

Stuttgart opens on Wednesday 25 at 18:00. Baden-Baden and Ravenna open on Thursday 26, and Europa-Park's winter season on Saturday 28.
